Matthew J. Liu

I'm an ML Researcher at a stealth startup in San Francisco, developing risk models for emerging markets. Previously, I was an Arnold O. Beckman Postdoctoral Fellow in Chemical Sciences at Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory with the Materials Project. There, I developed transformer models trained on physics-based simulations for geoscience. I also extended the Materials Project database to disordered materials by running high-throughput density functional theory calculations on high-performance computing clusters.

Before Berkeley Lab, I was a Technology Venture Fellow at Engine Ventures, an MIT-founded deep-tech venture firm, and a PhD Resident at Google X, Alphabet's moonshot lab. I completed my PhD in Chemical Engineering at Stanford University as a NASA NSTGRO and ARCS Fellow.

Workshop Papers

Simulated petrological labels enable probabilistic mineralogy prediction from whole-rock geochemistry

Matthew J. Liu, A. D. Kaplan, M. L. Whittaker, K. A. Persson

Sim2Science Workshop @ NeurIPS, 2026

arXivcodeproject overview

Sim-to-real for geoscience. We show that a transformer trained only on thermodynamic simulations transfers to real volcanic rocks, predicting mineral abundances as probability distributions.

Scaling laws for grid-based approximate nearest neighbor search in high dimensions

Matthew J. Liu, W. H. Zheng, V. Purohit, S. Xie, C. Li, N. Flynn

High-dimensional Learning Dynamics Workshop @ ICML, 2026

arXivcodeproject overview

Vector search methods underlie semantic search and RAG pipelines, yet grid-based methods have remained absent from modern scaling studies. We show that a multiprobe grid algorithm scales better with embedding dimension than graph-, tree-, and partition-based methods.
